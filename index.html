01 · about

Five years building the layer between raw warehouse tables and the people — and now the systems — asking questions of them. Snowflake, dbt, Airflow, SQL, Python, with CI-tested models and version-controlled releases.

The interesting problem right now isn't generating an answer. It's whether the answer is correct, permitted, and traceable. That means metrics defined once instead of five ways, access enforced at the boundary rather than trusted, and an evaluation set that tells you what percentage of questions the system actually gets right.

I came to this from the BI side, which is where you learn that "revenue" means four different things depending on who's asking. That ambiguity is exactly what breaks AI answers on enterprise data, and tooling doesn't solve it on its own.

Semantic & metrics layer

Metric definitions that live in version control, not in eleven dashboards that quietly disagree.

Analytics engineering

Modular ETL/ELT with dbt, Snowflake, and Airflow — tested, versioned, trusted downstream.

Evaluation & accuracy

Known-answer test sets, execution accuracy on generated SQL, and a written taxonomy of failures.

Governance & access

Row and column policies, masking, and audit logs that answer who asked what and what it touched.
